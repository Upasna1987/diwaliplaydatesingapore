Easy rangoli ideas for toddlers and preschoolers

Rangoli is one of the easiest ways to bring Deepavali home, but the classic coloured-powder kind and a two-year-old are not a great mix. Diwali Playdate 2026 had more than 100 guests: 45-plus kids aged 1 to 10 and 55-plus adults. Running rangoli for that many little ones taught us quickly what works for small hands and what ends up on the carpet. Here are the ideas we'd use again, sorted by age.

The short version
  • Under 2: flower petals only, on a big board or tray. No small grains.
  • 2 to 4: fill a chalk outline with coloured rice, dal or petals. Keep the shapes big.
  • 4 and up: stencils, symmetry, and a proper floor rangoli with parents helping.
  • Skip coloured powder with toddlers. It's messy and it stains hands.

What is rangoli, in one line for kids?

Rangoli is a bright pattern made at the doorway during Deepavali to welcome guests and good luck into the home. Families make them with coloured rice, flowers, chalk or powder, and every one is different.

Babies and young toddlers (under 2): flower-petal rangoli

For our under-2s we laid a large board on the floor and let them place soft flower petals: marigold, rose and chrysanthemum petals from the florist or a Little India garland shop. Petals are big enough to be safe if they go in a mouth, they feel lovely, and the result looks festive even when it's completely random.

  • Pull the petals off a few garlands into separate bowls by colour.
  • Draw one big circle with chalk or masking tape so there's a "place" to put them.
  • Let them drop, sort and scatter. That's the whole activity, and it can hold them for a surprisingly long time.

Avoid rice, dal, beads and sequins for this age. They are choking hazards, and it's the main reason we kept the baby rangoli to flowers only.

Toddlers (2 to 4): fill-the-shape rangoli

Two- and three-year-olds love filling a shape more than designing one. Draw a simple outline, a flower or a diya, then give them one material per section.

  • Coloured rice: mix uncooked rice with a little food colouring and a few drops of vinegar in a zip bag, shake, and dry it on a tray overnight. It's cheap, bright and easy to sweep up.
  • Dal and lentils: yellow moong, orange masoor, green moong and black urad give you four colours from the kitchen cupboard.
  • Glue version: for less sweeping, paint glue inside the outline on card first, then sprinkle. You get a keepsake to stick on the door.

Keep each section large, about the size of your child's palm. Small sections are frustrating at this age.

Preschoolers (4 to 6): colouring sheets and stencils

From around four, kids start to enjoy patterns and symmetry. Printable rangoli colouring sheets are a good bridge: they can colour them, or put the sheet in a clear plastic sleeve or tray and fill the shapes with rice and petals for a "real" rangoli they can redo again and again.

We made a free set of rangoli colouring sheets with seven designs, from a chunky toddler flower to a detailed mandala, plus a finish-the-other-half page and a dot-rangoli grid for kids who want to design their own.

Older kids (6 and up): a proper floor rangoli

This is what we'll do at the next Diwali Playdate. Draw a big pattern on the floor in chalk, at least a metre across, and mark each zone with a dab of washable paint to show its colour. Older kids fill the zones with coloured rice, dal or flowers while parents help. Put a plastic sheet down first and choose a low-traffic corner, because finished rangolis get trampled fast.

What we'd skip

In 2026 we tried small MDF rangoli boards with coloured powder. Honest review: the boards were too small, the powder went everywhere, and it stained little hands. Lovely for adults, not for under-5s. If you do want powder, use it outdoors, with older kids, on a big surface.

Quick safety checklist

  • Flowers only for babies and anyone who still mouths things.
  • Supervise grains and lentils with toddlers.
  • Keep diyas and candles well away from rangoli made with dry petals or paper.
  • Use food colouring or washable paint, not industrial dyes.
