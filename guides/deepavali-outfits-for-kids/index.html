Deepavali outfits for kids: what to wear and where to buy in Singapore

Deepavali is the one day of the year when kids are actively encouraged to sparkle. At Diwali Playdate 2026, every child got their moment on the runway at our best-dressed fashion show, and we saw everything from tiny silk pavadais to sharp kurtas and sequinned lehengas. This guide covers what kids traditionally wear, what actually works in Singapore's heat, how to dress babies and toddlers, footwear and accessories, and where to buy kids' Indian ethnic wear in Singapore.

The short version
  • Boys: kurta pyjama, kurta with dhoti pants, or a kurta with a Nehru jacket for something dressier.
  • Girls: lehenga choli, anarkali, salwar kameez or a South Indian pattu pavadai.
  • Babies: soft cotton kurta sets or festive rompers. Comfort first, sparkle second.
  • Choose cotton or cotton-silk for Singapore's heat, and bright festive colours.
  • Buy in Little India (Mustafa Centre, Tekka Place, Serangoon and Buffalo Road) or order online 2 to 3 weeks ahead.

What do kids wear for Deepavali?

There's no single “correct” outfit. Kids usually wear new clothes for Deepavali, ideally in bright, festive colours, and what they wear depends on the family's region and taste. North Indian families often choose kurtas and lehengas, while many Tamil families in Singapore dress girls in a silk pattu pavadai and boys in a veshti or kurta. Mixed and non-Indian families are always welcome to join in. Wearing Indian clothes to a Deepavali celebration is seen as a lovely, respectful gesture.

Deepavali outfit ideas for boys

  • Kurta pyjama: a long tunic with soft, straight trousers. The easiest and most comfortable choice, and it comes in every price range.
  • Kurta with dhoti pants: ready-stitched dhoti-style trousers with a draped look and no tying needed. Very cute on toddlers.
  • Nehru jacket set: a sleeveless jacket over a plain kurta. It instantly looks dressy and is easy to take off when it gets hot.
  • Sherwani: a long, embroidered coat for weddings and big parties. Gorgeous but warm, so save it for air-conditioned venues.
  • Veshti and shirt: the South Indian white or cream cloth with a gold border, worn with a shirt. Look for ready-to-wear velcro versions for kids.

Deepavali outfit ideas for girls

  • Lehenga choli: a full, flared skirt with a short blouse and a dupatta (scarf). Made for twirling, and the favourite on our runway.
  • Pattu pavadai: the traditional South Indian silk skirt and blouse, usually with a contrasting gold border. Beautiful in photos.
  • Anarkali: a long, flared dress-style kurta with leggings. Easy to wear and very comfortable for active kids.
  • Salwar kameez or kurta set: a tunic with matching trousers. Practical for a long day of crafts and games.
  • Sharara or gharara: a kurta with wide, flowing trousers. Comfortable and trendy.
  • Saree for kids: ready-stitched mini sarees are available for older girls who want to dress like mum.

What should babies and toddlers wear?

  • Choose soft cotton with a cotton lining. Scratchy embroidery and sequins on the inside are the number one cause of meltdowns.
  • Easy access for nappy changes: elastic waists, snap buttons and onesie-style kurta sets.
  • Skip the dupatta for under-3s. It ends up on the floor, or in their mouth.
  • Pack a spare outfit. Between rangoli, sweets and paint, you will probably need it.
  • Bring a change for the car ride home, so they can sleep in something comfortable.

Staying cool in Singapore's heat

A Deepavali outfit that works in Delhi in November can be a lot in Singapore's humidity. A few tips from many sweaty playdates:

  • Fabric: pick cotton, linen, cotton-silk or chanderi. Avoid heavy velvet, brocade and polyester for outdoor or daytime events.
  • Layers: a light kurta under a jacket or a lehenga with a removable dupatta means you can take something off when it gets hot.
  • Lining: check that the lining is cotton, not synthetic.
  • Timing: dress them just before you leave, not an hour early.

Footwear and accessories

  • Juttis and mojaris: embroidered flat shoes that finish an outfit beautifully. Break them in at home first, because new ones can rub.
  • Kolhapuri sandals: leather sandals that are comfortable for running around.
  • Bangles: loved by girls and boys alike, but choose light, rounded ones without sharp edges, and avoid tiny pieces for toddlers.
  • Bindis: stick-on bindis are fun for older kids. Skip them for babies who put things in their mouths.
  • Mehendi: temporary mehendi tattoo stickers are a quick, kid-friendly alternative to real henna.
  • Hair: flowers, clips and a simple braid with a gajra (flower string) for girls.

What colours to wear for Deepavali

Bright, festive colours are the tradition: red, orange, yellow, pink, gold, green and royal blue. Many families avoid all-black outfits for festivals, and some avoid plain white, which can be associated with mourning in some traditions. If you're not sure, choose something bright with a touch of gold and you can't go wrong.

Where to buy kids' Indian ethnic wear in Singapore

  • Mustafa Centre (Syed Alwi Road): a 24-hour department store with a big ethnic wear section, including affordable kids' sets.
  • Tekka Place and Tekka Centre: several shops for Indian clothing, with kids' kurtas and lehengas, plus tailors for alterations.
  • Serangoon Road, Buffalo Road and Little India Arcade: many shops for sarees, kurtas, jewellery and accessories. Prices and quality vary, so browse a few.
  • The Deepavali festival bazaar: in the weeks before Deepavali, stalls around Campbell Lane sell outfits, bangles, decorations and sweets. Busy but fun, so go early in the evening with kids.
  • Online and made-to-order: many Singapore boutiques and Indian brands ship here. Allow 2 to 3 weeks for made-to-order outfits, and longer in the Deepavali rush.
  • Family and friends: grandparents visiting from India are a classic source. Ask early.

Stock and opening hours change, especially around Deepavali, so it's worth checking with a shop before you make a special trip.

How to save money on kids' Deepavali outfits

  • Size up: buy one size bigger, with adjustable waists, so it fits next year too.
  • Swap: kids outgrow festive wear fast. Organise a swap with friends or your playgroup.
  • Mix and match: one good kurta works with different trousers and jackets for years.
  • Buy after Deepavali: many shops discount festive wear after the season.

Dressing for a Deepavali party or fashion show

At Diwali Playdate we hold a best-dressed fashion show, and the kids who enjoy it most are the comfortable ones. Choose an outfit they can walk, twirl and dance in, practise a “runway walk” at home, and let them pick one accessory themselves. Confidence beats sequins every time. And bring your camera. Those runway photos are the ones families tell us they treasure.

Frequently asked questions

What should kids wear for Deepavali in Singapore?

Boys usually wear a kurta pyjama, a kurta with dhoti pants or a Nehru jacket set. Girls wear a lehenga choli, anarkali, salwar kameez or a South Indian pattu pavadai. Choose cotton or cotton-silk for Singapore's heat, in bright festive colours.

Can non-Indian kids wear Indian clothes for Deepavali?

Yes. Wearing Indian clothes to a Deepavali celebration is welcomed and seen as a respectful way to join the festivities. Every family is welcome at Diwali Playdate, whatever they wear.

Where can I buy kids' Indian clothes in Singapore?

In Little India, try Mustafa Centre, the shops at Tekka Place, and the stores along Serangoon Road and Buffalo Road. The Deepavali festival bazaar around Campbell Lane also has outfits in the weeks before the festival. Many boutiques sell online, but allow 2 to 3 weeks for made-to-order outfits.

What should a baby wear for their first Deepavali?

A soft cotton kurta set or a festive romper with an elastic waist or snap buttons for easy nappy changes. Skip dupattas, small sewn-on beads and stick-on bindis for babies.

What colours are good for Deepavali?

Bright, festive colours like red, orange, yellow, pink, gold, green and royal blue. Many families avoid all-black or plain white outfits for festivals.

How do I keep my child cool in Indian clothes in Singapore?

Choose cotton, linen or cotton-silk with a cotton lining, avoid heavy velvet and brocade, pick outfits with removable layers, and dress them just before you leave.
