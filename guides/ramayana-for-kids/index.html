The Ramayana story for kids: how to explain Diwali in 5 minutes

“Why do we light diyas?” is one of the best questions a child can ask at Deepavali, and the answer is a story kids love: a brave prince, a ten-headed villain, a golden deer, a flying chariot and an army of monkeys. Every year at Diwali Playdate Singapore we tell the Ramayana in about five minutes, on a big screen, to 45-plus kids aged 1 to 10 and their parents. This guide has the version that works, a who's-who of the characters, how to adapt it for each age, and games that make it stick.

The short version
  • Diwali celebrates Prince Ram coming home to Ayodhya after 14 years in the forest, after defeating the demon king Raavan.
  • People lit rows of clay lamps to welcome him home on a dark night. That's why we light diyas.
  • The big idea for kids: light wins over darkness, and good wins over evil.

What is the Ramayana?

The Ramayana is one of the great epics of India, traditionally credited to the sage Valmiki. It tells the life of Prince Ram, his wife Sita and his brother Lakshman, and their battle against the demon king Raavan. Over two thousand years it has been retold in many languages and versions across India and Southeast Asia, from the Tamil Kamba Ramayanam to the Thai Ramakien and Indonesian shadow-puppet plays. That is why your child may hear slightly different details from different grandparents. All of them are “right”.

For Diwali, kids only need one part of it: the journey that ends with Ram coming home and everyone lighting lamps.

The Ramayana story for kids, in 10 short parts

  1. The prince of Ayodhya. Long ago in the kingdom of Ayodhya, kind King Dashrath had four sons. The eldest, Ram, was brave and gentle, and everyone in the kingdom loved him.
  2. The two wishes. Queen Kaikeyi wanted her own son, Bharat, to be king. She reminded King Dashrath of two promises he once made her, and asked that Bharat be crowned and that Ram live in the forest for 14 years. The king was heartbroken, but a promise is a promise.
  3. Into the forest. Ram didn't argue. He kept his father's promise and left the palace. His wife Sita and his loyal brother Lakshman chose to go with him. They built a little hut and lived simply among the trees and animals.
  4. The golden deer. In faraway Lanka, the ten-headed demon king Raavan made a wicked plan to take Sita. He sent a demon disguised as a beautiful golden deer. “Please catch it for me!” said Sita, and Ram followed it deep into the forest. Later Lakshman went to help him.
  5. Sita is taken. With Sita alone, Raavan came to the hut dressed as a holy man asking for food. When kind Sita stepped out to give it, he grabbed her and flew away to Lanka in his flying chariot. Sita dropped her jewellery along the way so Ram could follow her.
  6. New friends. Ram was heartbroken, but he never gave up. On his search he met Hanuman, the strong and clever monkey, and a whole army of monkeys and bears who promised to help.
  7. Hanuman's giant leap. Hanuman made himself enormous and leapt right across the ocean to Lanka. He found Sita in a garden, gave her Ram's ring and told her, “Ram is coming.”
  8. The floating bridge. To reach Lanka, the monkeys built a bridge across the sea. They wrote Ram's name on every stone, and the stones floated! Even a tiny squirrel helped by carrying grains of sand.
  9. Good wins. Ram and Raavan fought a great battle. Raavan was strong, but Ram was brave and good, and with one special arrow he defeated Raavan. Sita was free at last.
  10. The first Diwali. After 14 years, Ram, Sita and Lakshman came home to Ayodhya on a dark, moonless night. To show them the way, everyone lit rows and rows of clay lamps. And that is how Diwali, the festival of lights, began.

How to tell the Ramayana by age

  • Ages 1 to 3: skip the plot. Point and name: “Ram is blue. Hanuman is the monkey. Raavan has ten heads! Let's count them.” End with lighting a diya together. Repetition is everything at this age, so tell the same short version every night of Deepavali week.
  • Ages 3 to 6: tell the 10 parts above with pictures, and pause for sound effects: the whoosh of the flying chariot, the splash of the floating stones, the twang of Ram's bow. Let them shout the last line: “And that's why we light diyas!”
  • Ages 6 to 10: let them retell it to you from the pictures, then talk about the bigger questions below. Older kids also enjoy the parts we skip for little ones, such as Bharat refusing the throne and placing Ram's sandals on it instead.

Is the Ramayana too scary for young kids?

It doesn't have to be. Children's versions leave out the most violent parts of the battle, and Raavan works best as a grumpy, show-off villain rather than a frightening one. If your toddler is sensitive, focus on the friendship between Ram and Hanuman, the floating bridge and the lamps at the end. Kids also find it reassuring that the story always ends with everyone safe and home.

Questions to ask after the story

  • How do you think Sita felt in Lanka? How did she stay brave?
  • Ram kept his promise even though it was hard. Have you ever kept a hard promise?
  • Hanuman was small next to the ocean, but he still jumped. When have you done something brave?
  • Everyone helped build the bridge, even the tiny squirrel. Why does working together matter?
  • Why do you think people chose lamps to welcome Ram home?

What kids can learn from the Ramayana

  • Keeping your word: Ram leaves his home rather than break his father's promise.
  • Loyalty: Lakshman and Sita choose to share Ram's hard times.
  • Courage: Sita stays brave in Lanka, and Hanuman leaps the ocean.
  • Teamwork: monkeys, bears and one small squirrel build a bridge together.
  • Good over evil: Raavan is strong and clever, but greed and pride don't win.

Make it stick: Ramayan games and activities

  • Knock the Raavan: stick Raavan faces on 10 paper cups (one for each head), stack a 4-3-2-1 pyramid and knock it down with a soft ball. It was the most popular game at our 2026 playdate. Print the faces from our free Knock the Raavan pack.
  • Ramayan magnetic tile puzzles: print Ram, Sita, Hanuman and friends, cut them into 2 or 4 squares and stick them on magnetic tiles. Toddlers rebuild the characters while you tell the story. Free printable here.
  • Act it out: one child is Ram, one is Hanuman, a parent is Raavan (it's always a parent). Cushions make an excellent bridge to Lanka.
  • Light the way home: after the story, line up diyas or battery tea lights from the front door to the living room, just like Ayodhya. Our diya painting guide shows how to decorate them first.
  • Draw the ten heads: give older kids a long strip of paper and ask them to draw all ten of Raavan's heads, each with a different expression.

Is Diwali only about the Ramayana?

No. Different families and regions celebrate Diwali for different reasons. Many honour Goddess Lakshmi, so homes are cleaned, decorated and lit to welcome her. In South India, and for many Tamil families in Singapore, Deepavali marks Lord Krishna's victory over the demon Narakasura. Jains, Sikhs and some Buddhists also mark the festival for their own reasons. For young kids, the Ramayana is the easiest story to start with, and the message is the same: light over darkness.

The Ramayana also connects to Dussehra (also called Vijayadashami), celebrated about three weeks before Diwali, which marks Ram's victory over Raavan. In North India, giant Raavan effigies are set alight that day, which is the idea behind our Knock the Raavan game.

Frequently asked questions

What is the story behind Diwali for kids?

Diwali celebrates Prince Ram returning home to Ayodhya with Sita and Lakshman after 14 years in the forest and his victory over the demon king Raavan. It was a dark night, so the people lit rows of clay lamps to welcome them. That's why we light diyas at Diwali.

How long does it take to tell the Ramayana to kids?

The short version in this guide takes about five minutes. For toddlers, a one-minute version that names the characters and ends with lighting a lamp works best.

What age is the Ramayana suitable for?

Any age, with the right version. Under-3s enjoy naming the characters in pictures, 3 to 6-year-olds love the 10-part story with sound effects, and kids from about 6 can retell it and discuss what it means.

Why does Raavan have ten heads?

In the story, Raavan's ten heads show how clever, powerful and proud he was. Many people also explain them as ten bad habits, such as greed, anger and jealousy, that Ram defeats. For kids, it's a great counting game.

Why is Ram blue?

Ram, like Krishna, is traditionally shown with blue skin. Blue is the colour of the sky and the ocean, and it's used to show that he is divine and endless. For young kids, it's also simply how to spot him in every picture.

What's the difference between Dussehra and Diwali?

Dussehra celebrates the day Ram defeated Raavan. Diwali, about three weeks later, celebrates Ram, Sita and Lakshman arriving home to Ayodhya, when everyone lit lamps to welcome them.
