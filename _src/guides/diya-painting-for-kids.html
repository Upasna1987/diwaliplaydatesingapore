What is a diya, and why do we light them?

A diya is a small oil lamp, traditionally made of clay, with a cotton wick. During Deepavali, families light rows of diyas at the door, on windowsills and around the home. The tradition remembers the night Ram, Sita and Lakshman came home to Ayodhya after 14 years, when everyone lit lamps to show them the way. Many families also light diyas to welcome Goddess Lakshmi, who brings good fortune. Deepavali literally means “row of lamps”.

For kids, the message is simple: a little light can chase away the dark. Our Ramayana story for kids tells the full tale in five minutes.

What you need

  • Plain clay diyas, unglazed, so the paint sticks. Shops around Little India and Tekka sell them in packs in the weeks before Deepavali, and you can also order them online.
  • Or glass tea-light holders. This is what we used at the playdate. They are sturdier than clay, easy to wipe, and look lovely lit up.
  • Acrylic paint or acrylic paint markers. Markers are far less messy for under-6s.
  • Decorations: self-adhesive gems, mirror stickers, glitter glue and washi tape.
  • Cotton buds and old paintbrushes for dots.
  • Newspaper or a plastic tablecloth, wet wipes and an old shirt or apron.
  • Tea lights: battery ones for toddlers, real ones for adults to light.

How to paint a diya: step by step

  1. Wipe the diya with a damp cloth to remove clay dust, and let it dry. Paint sticks much better to a clean surface.
  2. Paint a base colour on the outside and the rim. Yellow, orange, red and pink look the most festive. Leave the inside unpainted, because that's where the wick or tea light sits.
  3. Let it dry for about 20 minutes. This is a good moment for a snack, or for the Ramayana story.
  4. Add patterns: dots around the rim, little leaves, zigzags or a rangoli flower. Cotton buds and the back of a paintbrush make perfect dots.
  5. Decorate with gems, mirror stickers or glitter glue.
  6. Dry overnight before lighting. Paint near a flame needs to be completely dry.

10 easy diya decoration ideas for kids

  1. Dot border: a ring of white dots around the rim on a bright base. Easy and classic.
  2. Rainbow diya: each section a different colour, perfect for toddlers who want to use everything.
  3. Mirror sparkle: mirror stickers all round, so the flame bounces light everywhere.
  4. Paisley: the teardrop paisley shape from mehendi designs, in gold on red.
  5. Mini rangoli: a small flower painted on the side of the diya.
  6. Peacock: blue and green with “feather eyes” made of dots.
  7. Washi stripes: strips of patterned tape around a tea-light holder. Zero paint.
  8. Gold and white: a simple, elegant combination for older kids.
  9. Name diya: each family member's name on their own diya.
  10. Thumbprint diya: toddler thumbprints as petals around the rim. A lovely keepsake.

Diya painting by age

  • Ages 1 to 3: a glass tea-light holder, big stickers or one chunky paint marker. Skip small gems and sequins, which are choking hazards. The goal is colour, not neatness.
  • Ages 3 to 6: acrylic paint markers and self-adhesive gems. They can manage a base colour plus dots, with some help.
  • Ages 6 to 10: real acrylic paint, fine brushes and patterns. Older kids love copying mehendi or rangoli designs onto the rim.

How to make your own diya with air-dry clay

Making the diya from scratch turns one craft into two sessions, which is perfect for a long weekend.

  1. Take a ball of air-dry clay about the size of a lime.
  2. Press your thumb into the middle and pinch the sides up to make a small bowl.
  3. Pinch one side into a point for the wick spout, the classic diya shape.
  4. Smooth it with a wet finger and press in patterns with a fork, a toothpick or a bottle cap.
  5. Leave it to dry for 24 to 48 hours, then paint it as above.

Air-dry clay diyas are best used with a battery tea light. They aren't fired, so they shouldn't hold hot oil or a real flame.

Lighting diyas safely with kids

  • Put a tea light inside instead of oil and a cotton wick. It's cleaner and steadier.
  • For toddlers and preschoolers, use battery tea lights. They glow beautifully and can be carried around.
  • An adult lights any real flame, and the diya sits on a heatproof plate, away from curtains, rangoli made with dry petals, and little hands.
  • Keep paint and glitter on the outside only. Never paint inside the cup.
  • Wear fitted sleeves and tie back long hair when lighting. Flowing festive outfits and flames don't mix.

Make it part of Deepavali

Let kids place their diya at the front door next to a rangoli, or carry it during the evening puja. If you're making several, line them up along the windowsill. It's the closest thing to Ayodhya in an HDB flat. Painted diyas also make lovely gifts for grandparents, teachers and neighbours.

Frequently asked questions

What paint should kids use on clay diyas?

Acrylic paint or acrylic paint markers work best on unglazed clay. Poster and tempera paints are washable but tend to flake off clay. Let the paint dry overnight before lighting.

Can you light a painted diya?

Yes, as long as only the outside is painted and the paint is completely dry. Use a tea light inside, place the diya on a heatproof plate, and always have an adult light it.

What age can kids paint diyas?

From about two, with stickers and a chunky paint marker on a glass tea-light holder. From three to six, paint markers and gems. From six, acrylic paint and detailed patterns.

Where can I buy diyas in Singapore?

Shops around Little India, Tekka and the Deepavali festival bazaar sell plain clay diyas in packs in the weeks before Deepavali. Glass tea-light holders are available from homeware shops all year round.

How long does a painted diya take to dry?

Acrylic paint is touch-dry in about 20 to 30 minutes, but leave it overnight before lighting. Air-dry clay diyas need 24 to 48 hours to dry before painting.

How do I make diya painting less messy?

Use acrylic paint markers instead of brushes, cover the table with a plastic cloth, give each child a small amount of paint on a paper plate, and keep wet wipes close by. Stickers and washi tape are completely mess-free.
