“Why do we light diyas?” is one of the best questions a child can ask at Deepavali, and the answer is a story kids love: a brave prince, a ten-headed villain, a flying chariot and an army of monkeys. Every year at Diwali Playdate Singapore we tell the Ramayana in about five minutes, on a big screen, to 45-plus kids aged 1 to 10 and their parents. Here is the version that works, plus how to make it stick.

The short version
  • Diwali celebrates Prince Ram coming home to Ayodhya after 14 years, after defeating the demon king Raavan.
  • People lit rows of clay lamps to welcome him. That's why we light diyas.
  • The big idea for kids: light wins over darkness, and good wins over evil.

The Ramayana story for kids, in 10 short parts

  1. Long ago in the kingdom of Ayodhya, kind King Dashrath had four sons. The eldest, Ram, was brave and gentle.
  2. Queen Kaikeyi wanted her own son Bharat to be king, so she asked the king to send Ram to live in the forest for 14 years.
  3. Ram kept his father's promise and left. His wife Sita and his loyal brother Lakshman went with him.
  4. In the forest, the ten-headed demon king Raavan of Lanka made a wicked plan. He sent a beautiful golden deer to lure Ram away.
  5. While Ram and Lakshman were gone, Raavan came disguised as a holy man and carried Sita away to Lanka in his flying chariot.
  6. Ram was heartbroken, but he never gave up. On his search he met Hanuman, the strong and clever monkey, and a whole army of monkeys and bears.
  7. Hanuman leapt across the ocean and found Sita in Lanka.
  8. The monkeys built a bridge of stones across the sea. They wrote Ram's name on every stone, and the stones floated!
  9. Ram fought Raavan in a great battle and won. Good won over evil.
  10. Ram, Sita and Lakshman came home to Ayodhya on a dark, moonless night, and everyone lit diyas to show them the way. That was the first Diwali.

How to tell it by age

  • Ages 1 to 3: skip the plot. Point and name: “Ram is blue. Hanuman is the monkey. Raavan has ten heads! Count them.” End with lighting a diya together.
  • Ages 3 to 6: tell the 10 parts above with pictures, and pause for sound effects: the whoosh of the flying chariot, the splash of the floating stones.
  • Ages 6 to 10: let them retell it to you from the pictures, then ask the bigger questions below.

Questions to ask after the story

  • How do you think Sita felt in Lanka? How did she stay brave?
  • Hanuman was small next to the ocean, but he still jumped. When have you done something brave?
  • Everyone helped build the bridge, even the tiny squirrels. Why does working together matter?
  • Why do you think people chose lamps to welcome Ram home?

Make it stick: three Ramayan games

  • Knock the Raavan: stick Raavan faces on 10 paper cups (one for each head), stack a 4-3-2-1 pyramid and knock it down with a soft ball. It was the most popular game at our 2026 playdate. Print the faces from our free Knock the Raavan pack.
  • Ramayan magnetic tile puzzles: print Ram, Sita, Hanuman and friends, cut them into 2 or 4 squares and stick them on magnetic tiles. Toddlers rebuild the characters while you tell the story. Free printable here.
  • Act it out: one child is Ram, one is Hanuman, a parent is Raavan (it's always a parent). Cushions make an excellent bridge to Lanka.

Is Diwali only about the Ramayana?

No. Different families and regions celebrate Diwali for different reasons. Many also honour Goddess Lakshmi, so homes are cleaned and lit to welcome her, and in some traditions it marks Lord Krishna's victory over the demon Narakasura. For young kids, the Ramayana is the easiest story to start with, and the message is the same: light over darkness.
